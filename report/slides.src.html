2 · The problem

Neighbors want to help neighbors.
There's no bridge.

The giving tree. Growing up, my church hung paper ornaments with wishes from people in our community. A neighbor took one and made it come true. I always wondered: why only at Christmas?

The winter drive. Running a soup kitchen's clothing drive, I saw people want to help the moment they knew someone nearby needed it.

Lasagna Love. Matching neighbors with someone close by to cook them a meal showed me local, personal giving works, all year.

10 hours How fast Memphis's Angel Tree filled, after in-person appointments with ID, birth certificates and proof of income. Augusta's closed in under 24.
Over 60% of New Haven households can't afford a basic survival budget. About 40% across Connecticut, a record 581,000.

Our three differences

  • Fairness by design. One neutral format for every wish; ranked on urgency and waiting time, never on storytelling.
  • AI does the hard part. The intake interview and the privacy review are exactly what forces others into appointments and caseworkers.
  • Year-round and local. One hub, one weekly delivery, every kind of need.
The honest gap: no partner verification yet. Everyone else on that list has one.

4 · How it works

Three agents, one path

Intake — turns plain English into wishes you approve.
Privacy & Safety — the only route to the tree. Removes anything identifying.
Matchmaker — ranks on urgency and waiting time, never on wording.

Code re-checks every rule the agents are asked to follow. A prompt is a request; code is a promise.

6 · Cost to run

The AI is the cheap part

$0.0013 per neighbor journey
intake + privacy review
$0.0005 per wish granter journey
one Matchmaker run
$10.56 a month at 10,000 users
every model call, everything
Kept there by explicit prompt caching, a 15-candidate limit, and turn and token caps — none of which cost us anything. More candidates returned the same five wishes in the same order. A 300-token output cap did bind, and broke 2 of 10 intakes outright: the cap is a trap, the prompt is the lever.
The real constraint is a person. Ten thousand users cost about eleven dollars of model calls a month — and need far more than the ~6 hours a week of admin time that one drop-off window and one delivery run already take.

7 · Safety

Privacy enforced in code

A neighbor typed a child's name, a named high school and a phone number. Published as "Twin bed frame" — and told exactly what went.

10 / 10 live attack tests passed against the real model — injection, PII, cash requests, scams, caps, passcode. $0.0039.

Two layersClosed vocabulary Caps server-sideNo contact details to agents

Wishes are data, never instructions. Python re-checks the agent and can only make a wish less public. The admin passcode and demo login shortcut are deliberately demo-grade.

9 · What's next

From demo to something a community relies on

Build

  • Partner mode, piloted with a soup kitchen's winter clothing drive — the drive I used to organize by hand.
  • Partner verification through referrers: schools, churches and pantries already know who is struggling.
  • Volunteer delivery, opt-in on both sides, so neighbors who want to meet can.

Grow

  • Wish granters through Instagram, student ambassadors and the SOM community.
  • Wishes through trusted partners — the same relationship that solves verification.
  • Community leads per region, the way Lasagna Love grew.
Nobody having a hard month goes looking for a giving website. They talk to their child's school.
